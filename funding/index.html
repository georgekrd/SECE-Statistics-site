def and_join(items) -> str:
    """'a', 'a and b', 'a, b and c'."""
    items = [str(item) for item in items]
    return items[0] if len(items) == 1 else ", ".join(items[:-1]) + " and " + items[-1]


def wilson(k, n, z: float = 1.96):
    """95% Wilson score interval of a proportion k/n (arrays allowed)."""
    k, n = np.asarray(k, dtype=float), np.asarray(n, dtype=float)
    p = k / n
    den = 1 + z**2 / n
    centre = (p + z**2 / (2 * n)) / den
    half = z * np.sqrt(p * (1 - p) / n + z**2 / (4 * n**2)) / den
    return centre - half, centre + half

_FUNDER_RX = [(re.compile(pattern), name, group) for pattern, name, group in FUNDER_RULES]


def fold(text: object) -> str:
    """Matching key: accents removed, lower case, unified apostrophes and spaces ('' if missing)."""
    if not isinstance(text, str):
        return ""
    text = unicodedata.normalize("NFKD", text.replace("’", "'"))
    text = "".join(ch for ch in text if not unicodedata.combining(ch))
    return re.sub(r"\s+", " ", text).strip().lower()


def classify_funder(name: object) -> tuple[str, str]:
    """(normalised funder, funder group) of one Scopus funding entry."""
    if not isinstance(name, str) or not name.strip():
        return "(no funder name)", UNCLASS
    clean = re.sub(r"\s+", " ", name.replace("’", "'")).strip()
    key = fold(clean)
    for rx, norm, group in _FUNDER_RX:
        if rx.search(key):
            return norm or clean, group
    return clean, UNCLASS

def publisher_group(name: object) -> str | None:
    """Publisher group of a raw Scopus Publisher value (the raw name for small publishers)."""
    if not isinstance(name, str) or not name.strip():
        return None
    low = fold(name)
    return next((group for group, rx in PUBLISHER_RX if rx.search(low)), name.strip())


def name_group(*names: object) -> str | None:
    """Publisher implied by a source title or conference name, if any."""
    for name in names:
        low = fold(name)
        for group, rx in NAME_RX:
            if low and rx.search(low):
                return group
    return None

Funding acknowledgements over time¶

Open access and funding¶

Funded publications are more often open access: in 2021–2025, 44.7% of the funded and 38.1% of the other publications were open. The gap holds among journal publications (65.7% against 54.4%) and within their two largest publishers (IEEE 55.0% against 47.5%, Elsevier 40.5% against 25.6%). It is largest for publications acknowledging an EU research programme: 78.2% of such journal publications were open in 2021–2025, up from 63.9% in 2016–2020 (Figure 7); Horizon 2020 and Horizon Europe require open access to the peer-reviewed publications of the projects they fund. Among conference papers only those acknowledging an EU research programme stand out (26.6% open, against 12.0% with other funding only and 15.7% without an acknowledgement).

These are associations, not effects: funded and unfunded publications also differ in topic, venue and collaboration, and the comparison does not control for these factors.

Notes & caveats¶

  • Funding data. Scopus extracts funder names and grant numbers from the acknowledgements of the papers. The data are incomplete: a missing entry does not prove that a publication was unfunded. Funding details are particularly sparse for older conference papers: in the five-year periods from 2001 to 2015, Scopus lists funders for at most 8.7% of the conference papers, against 21.1%–30.3% of the journal publications. Counts are publications that acknowledge a funder, not amounts of money or numbers of grants.

  • Normalisation. The 1,134 distinct funder names were mapped by rules to normalised names (spelling variants, translations, programme and agency names) and to seven groups. Entries that Scopus files under the wrong body are moved to the funder meant when the data show which one it is. Scopus records the Greek RESEARCH – CREATE – INNOVATE action as the Australian agency “Create NSW” (27 publications) or as the fragments “RESEARCH” and “CREATE”; one “Create NSW” entry is in fact the CREATE programme of Canada’s NSERC. The “College of Environmental Science and Forestry” of the State University of New York (10 publications), filed under the acronym ESF, is the European Social Fund. The European Science Foundation (3 publications since 2015), also filed under ESF, is the European Social Fund or, with a COST grant, a COST action. The Spanish rare-disease federation FEDER (3) is the European Regional Development Fund (FEDER in Spanish), and Xi’an Eurasia University (1), filed under the acronym EU, is the European Union. Publications of RESEARCH – CREATE – INNOVATE are found by these names or by the action’s project codes (T1EDK, T2EDK). Horizon 2020 entries on publications from before 2014, when the programme began, count as FP7. Some entries name a body abroad, or a ministry without its country, where the other funders, grant numbers or affiliations of the publication point to another funder, in most cases a Greek or EU one: these 14 entries on 14 publications are moved to that funder. Spain’s “Ministerio de Fomento” (3 publications), for example, is the Greek Ministry of Development; the Swedish Foundation for Strategic Research stands for IKY, the State Scholarships Foundation (both “SSF”), in 2 of its entries; and Canada’s AUTO21 network (2) is the FP6 network of excellence listed with it. The sample acknowledgement of the IEEE paper template, to the U.S. Department of Commerce, is left unclassified, and so are entries whose funder cannot be identified, among them “UK Research and Innovation” on a 2003 publication, before UKRI was founded in 2018, and a ministry that names no country as the only funder of a 1996 publication with affiliations in Greece only. 2.7% of the funding entries remain unclassified (mostly project names, fragments and 21 entries without a funder name); 11 funded publications have no classified funder.

  • Groups. EU research programmes: the framework programmes up to Horizon Europe, including the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, the ERC, COST and other programmes managed by the European Commission. EU structural & recovery funds: the European Social Fund, the European Regional Development Fund, the Greek NSRF/ESPA operational programmes that these funds co-finance with national funds (among them RESEARCH – CREATE – INNOVATE), Interreg and NextGenerationEU; the national share of the co-financing is not separated. Greek public funders: HFRI, GSRT, IKY, ministries and other public bodies. Other public funders: the national and regional public funders of other countries, EU member states included, and international bodies (for example NSFC, EPSRC, NSF, QNRF, Portugal’s FCT, Germany’s DFG and NATO). Universities & research centres include AUTH’s own funds and Khalifa University; Industry includes the Public Power Corporation and OTE, and state-owned companies abroad such as the Dubai Electricity and Water Authority.

  • The European Commission entry. Scopus often lists “European Commission” next to a programme or fund that the Commission manages. These entries are merged into the EU programme or fund named on the same publication (449 publications); on the other 106 publications no programme is named and the entry counts with the EU research programmes.

  • Open-access flags. Scopus takes its open-access flags from Unpaywall. They describe the status on 2 November 2026, not at publication, and change as embargoes end and repositories are indexed. As a check, all 120 journal publications of 2021–2025 from MDPI and Frontiers, which publish only open-access journals, are flagged as open.

  • Partial year. The 2026 records (to 2 November 2026) count in the funder totals but not in the shares by year or period, the funding mix or the comparisons.

  • Associations. Differences between groups of publications (funded or not, source types, publishers) are descriptive; they do not show that funding or a publisher causes open access, or the reverse.

  • Small groups. The page gives no number of papers that are international only through SECE authors’ own second affiliations abroad, since such a number can describe one or two people; it describes these papers in words instead. This also covers numbers that could be worked out from the figures shown, such as the difference between two shares of the same publications with and without these affiliations.
